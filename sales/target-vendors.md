### 1. Regional Food & Beverage Distributors
**Score: 19/20 · Tier 1**

**SCORE RATIONALE**
A single restaurant account is worth $50K–$500K/year in food purchases. Reps find new accounts by driving their territory and hoping to spot a new restaurant sign — there is no systematic approach in any regional distributor's playbook.

**THEIR CURRENT PAIN**
A sales rep drives the same route every week. They find out a new restaurant opened when they see the lights on. By then Sysco has already been in twice and Labatt's territory manager has a trial order running. Every account they win after opening is a displacement sale — harder, slower, and often impossible if the operator is already happy. The representative who gets there first at a new restaurant almost always keeps the account for years. They are losing those accounts every month and don't know it.

**THE ANGLE**
"Phoebe's Diner opened their fourth Austin location last month. I have the name, phone, and email of the partner who handles procurement. I know about four more openings in the next 90 days that aren't in any news coverage yet. I want to show you what that looks like for 15 minutes."

**WHAT THIS SOLVES FOR THEM**
Your rep finds out about a new restaurant when they see the sign go up — by then Sysco already has the account. A 30,000 square foot restaurant is being built at Domain Northside right now with no public announcement; the first distributor to reach the operator sets the opening order. Bob Woody is adding venue number 29 on Rainey Street and his personal cell phone is on his website — the distributor who calls him gets added to a 20-venue portfolio, not a single account.

**HERO LEAD FOR THIS CATEGORY**
Red Car (Austin) at Domain Northside. 30,160 SF. No press, no TABC, no public announcement. A restaurant this size will need a primary distributor relationship in place months before opening to source ingredients, negotiate pricing, and set up delivery cadences. The operator is currently unknown — but the GC's phone number is confirmed. First distributor to make that call is the only distributor in the room.

**DISQUALIFIERS**
National procurement teams (Sysco, US Foods) who don't let territory managers make subscription decisions. Distributors with a single product line (dairy only, bread only) who can't service a full restaurant account. Any buyer who needs committee approval — this must be a sales manager or owner who closes accounts themselves.

### 2. Commercial Insurance Brokers — Restaurant Lines
**Score: 18/20 · Tier 1**

**SCORE RATIONALE**
An independent broker decides in a day, no committee. Their entire business is finding clients before a competitor quotes them — this product is a direct match for how they already think. Value per client is moderate on a single restaurant but scales sharply with portfolio operators.

**THEIR CURRENT PAIN**
Insurance brokers find new restaurant clients through referrals, LinkedIn cold outreach, and calling on restaurants they drive past that look new. A pre-opening restaurant is the ideal prospect — no incumbent policy, open to all carriers, actively buying. But brokers have no way to identify pre-opening restaurants systematically. By the time the sign is up and the doors are open, a general agent or a referral from the GC often already has the quote in front of the owner. The broker who gets there at permit stage has no competition.

**THE ANGLE**

I also have Bob Woody's personal cell — he owns 20 Austin bars and his portfolio is worth a conversation that takes 10 minutes."

**WHAT THIS SOLVES FOR THEM**
By the time a broker finds out a restaurant opened, the policy is already placed — this gets them in at permit stage, before any carrier has quoted. Bob Woody's 20-venue portfolio is the kind of account that transforms a commercial lines book; the broker who reaches him during a new build has a reason to call that nobody else has. Pre-announcement leads like the Domain Northside project give brokers a prospect list that no referral network, directory, or cold list can replicate.

**HERO LEAD FOR THIS CATEGORY**
Bob Woody portfolio (Rainey Tavern). He owns 20–30 Austin bars. His personal cell and personal email are self-published on bobwoody.com. He is building a new bar on Rainey Street right now. A broker who calls him during the build has a specific reason to be on the phone — and if they win the Rainey Tavern policy, they have a natural conversation about every other venue in the portfolio. This is not a $15K policy. This is a $200K+ commercial portfolio relationship.

**DISQUALIFIERS**
National agency employees who can't make their own client decisions. Brokers who don't actively write hospitality lines — liquor liability experience is required, not optional. Any agency with a 90-day committee sales process is the wrong buyer.

### 3. Grease Trap & Waste Oil Services
**Score: 18/20 · Tier 1**

**SCORE RATIONALE**
Every commercial kitchen is required by code to have grease trap service before opening. There is no opt-out. These operators have extreme hunger because they literally cannot find new accounts until the health department calls them or a restaurant owner Googles them after the trap backs up.

**THEIR CURRENT PAIN**
Grease trap companies find new restaurant clients one of three ways: the health department calls them for an emergency, the restaurant Googles "grease trap service Austin" after a problem, or a technician on a service call mentions the company to a neighboring business. None of these happen before opening. Every new restaurant signs with whoever is fastest and cheapest when the kitchen is already running. The company that knows about a new kitchen before the doors open never has to compete on price.

**THE ANGLE**
"There are six kitchens in active buildout in Austin right now that are each required to install a grease trap before they can get a health permit. Five of them have no service company. I know the addresses and the permit timelines."

**WHAT THIS SOLVES FOR THEM**
New kitchen builds with exterior grease trap installation in the permit description are a guaranteed future service account — this brief identifies them 6–12 months before they open. The Red Car project at Domain Northside (30,160 SF) will require commercial grease trap infrastructure at a scale most Austin kitchens never approach; the service company who reaches the operator during build signs a contract with no competition. Pre-announcement leads mean no price-shopping — the operator hasn't had time to get three quotes.

**HERO LEAD FOR THIS CATEGORY**
Red Car (Austin), 11821 Rock Rose Ave. Permit description: "Change of Use from Retail to Restaurant — 30,160 SF." A kitchen at this scale requires commercial grease interceptor infrastructure, not a standard trap. The grease removal contract for this account will be significantly larger than a typical restaurant. Zero public announcement. Zero competing proposals. The GC is Trinity Constructors at (512) 576-0636.

**DISQUALIFIERS**
Operators who only pump residential septic and don't service commercial kitchens. Companies without a commercial kitchen compliance program — FOG (Fats, Oils, Grease) regulations require documentation the restaurant will need for health inspections. PE-backed companies where local operators don't make their own pricing decisions.

### 5. POS System Resellers
**Score: 17/20 · Tier 1**

**SCORE RATIONALE**
A POS installation is $5–15K hardware plus $2–5K/year recurring SaaS. The operator who gets a POS demo first, in a convenient time before opening chaos, almost always closes. Regional dealers who sell Revel, Lightspeed, or Focus POS make their own account decisions and are hungry for leads.

**THEIR CURRENT PAIN**
Regional POS dealers find restaurant prospects through Google Ads, referrals from other vendors, and cold calling restaurants they see opening nearby. By the time a restaurant is staffed and training, the POS decision is 90% made — often by whoever got in first during the build-out. Toast sends direct sales reps who call on new restaurants immediately after they surface in any public database. A regional Lightspeed or Revel dealer can't compete on speed unless they have the lead before Toast does.

**THE ANGLE**
"Phoebe's Diner is running four Austin locations on a POS system they chose when they had one location in 2017. They just opened their fourth and are looking at a fifth. Nobody has asked them to upgrade. I have the direct contacts for both the culinary partner and the business partner."

**WHAT THIS SOLVES FOR THEM**
Pre-announcement leads give a regional POS dealer a demo appointment before Toast's reps know a restaurant exists. Multi-location operators like Phoebe's Diner (4 locations) or the Hajimaleki group (7 venues) represent enterprise-scale POS contracts, not single installs — and these operators are accessible right now. A new opening with no POS system yet is the only moment when the field is perfectly level between a national brand rep and a local dealer.

**HERO LEAD FOR THIS CATEGORY**
Phoebe's Diner (Winebelly Group). Four locations, all Austin, opened their fourth in April 2026. They have been on the same POS since 2017. A regional POS dealer who presents a multi-location upgrade before the fifth location opens has the strongest possible business case: unified reporting, loyalty program, consolidated menu management across all four sites. Camden Stuerzenberger (Chef/Partner) and Eric Tran (Co-Founder) are both reachable with confirmed contact data.

**DISQUALIFIERS**

### 14. Payroll & HR Providers — Regional
**Score: 12/20 · Tier 2**

**SCORE RATIONALE**
Restaurant groups with 3+ locations are the real target — single-location restaurants often handle payroll with a basic SaaS tool. Value per client is modest unless the operator grows. Speed is slower because payroll transitions require a 30-day parallel run, which means the sales cycle is longer than most other categories.

**THEIR CURRENT PAIN**
Regional payroll providers find restaurant clients through accounting referrals, Chamber of Commerce networking, and cold calling. The challenge is that most restaurants set up payroll before opening with whatever their bookkeeper recommends, and switching payroll providers is painful — operators only do it when they have a serious problem. Pre-opening is the only frictionless moment. Multi-location operators who are growing are the best prospects because each new location adds headcount to an existing relationship.

**THE ANGLE**
"Phoebe's Diner just opened their fourth Austin location and they've been adding employees with each one. A payroll provider who reaches the business operations partner before location five opens has a natural conversation about multi-location consolidation."

**WHAT THIS SOLVES FOR THEM**
Restaurant payroll has specific compliance complexity — tip credit accounting, FLSA overtime, multiple state filings for multi-city groups — that most general payroll providers handle poorly. A regional provider with restaurant-specific expertise has a differentiation story. Multi-location operators represent compounding revenue with each new opening.

**HERO LEAD FOR THIS CATEGORY**
Phoebe's Diner / Winebelly Group. Four locations, all Austin, actively expanding. Each new location adds kitchen staff, servers, and management. The business operations partner (Eric Tran, eric@austinwinebelly.com inferred) handles contracts and vendor relationships. **DISQUALIFIERS**
ADP national sales team — not a regional relationship. Payroll providers without restaurant-specific tip credit and FICA tip tax experience. Any provider who can't demo a multi-location dashboard.

### 15. Music Licensing & Sound System Installation
**Score: 12/20 · Tier 2**

**SCORE RATIONALE**
Sound system installation is a one-time project sale; ASCAP/BMI licensing management is recurring but low-ticket. Hunger is real — restaurant owners are regularly fined for playing unlicensed music — but this is not a category where one new account transforms a vendor's business. Timing is critical because sound systems are specified during buildout.

**THEIR CURRENT PAIN**
Sound system installers find restaurant clients through GC referrals, architect specs, and AV design firms. ASCAP/BMI consultants find clients through industry events and cold calling. Restaurants that open without proper licensing are hit with compliance notices within 60–90 days and then need to deal with it reactively. A vendor who reaches the operator during the build phase has both the sound system install and the licensing conversation.

**THE ANGLE**
"There are seven restaurants currently in buildout in Austin that will each need a sound system specified before they open. The operator opening at Domain Northside has 30,000 square feet of service floor to wire. I have the GC."

**WHAT THIS SOLVES FOR THEM**
Sound systems specified during construction are included in the build budget — not a discretionary purchase after opening. Large format restaurants like the Domain project require distributed audio architecture that must be planned before walls close. ASCAP/BMI licensing is a compliance requirement with penalties — the vendor who gets in early builds a long-term relationship with the operator.

**HERO LEAD FOR THIS CATEGORY**
Red Car (Austin), 30,160 SF. A restaurant/entertainment venue at this scale requires a professional distributed audio installation — not a Sonos setup. The project is currently in active buildout with Trinity Constructors. A commercial AV company who calls the GC with a sound system proposal has a specific, time-bounded reason to be in the conversation.

**DISQUALIFIERS**
Residential AV installers without commercial installation credentials. ASCAP/BMI representatives who only handle compliance — not relevant without an installation capability. Any vendor who can't deliver a professional AV specification to an architect or GC.
